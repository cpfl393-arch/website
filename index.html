PhD student in Political Science at the Bush School of Government and Public Service. I study political psychology, electoral accountability, and the moments when partisanship and moral judgment pull in opposite directions.

The question

Democratic accountability assumes voters can recognize when something has gone wrong — and respond. My work asks when that assumption holds, and when it quietly fails.

Background

Before Texas A&M

I hold an M.A. in Political Science from Seoul National University, where my thesis examined how voters weighed normative issues against partisanship in the 2021 South Korean by-elections. I worked as a research associate at the Yunposun Institute for Democracy, the Institute for Future Strategy at Seoul National University, and the Korean Women's Development Institute — designing survey instruments, validating data, and turning findings into work that people outside the academy could read.

I have also spent three national election cycles doing field research in Korean districts, which is where most of my questions actually come from.
